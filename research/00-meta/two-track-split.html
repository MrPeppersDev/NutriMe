2. The two pieces & where they meet

Track G — Nutrition Guidance is everything that touches health: screener intake (PHQ-2 / GAD-2 / Hunger Vital Sign), demographics + life-stage + allergens, the clinical side of the knowledge model, DRI/life-stage constraint derivation, allergen & condition gating, evidence tiers, honest-disclosure content, and the constitutional safety rules. It carries all the PHI and answers "what does healthy eating look like for this person, and why."

Track M — Meal-Planning & Grocery is the convenience app: the recipe corpus and its ingest adapters, pantry/fridge/freezer inventory, meal-plan assembly, grocery list + Instacart, cooking-time feedback, and the daily home surface. It carries no health data of its own and answers "what's for dinner, what do I buy, when do I start cooking."

TRACK G — Nutrition Guidance screeners · clinical atoms · DRI/life-stage constraints · allergen & condition gating · evidence tiers · safety rules
↓ distills into ↓ ABSTRACTED CONSTRAINTS
"avoids X" · "prefers Y" · "prioritize nutrient Z" · "hard-block W" — never raw scores or diagnoses ↓ consumed by ↓
TRACK M — Meal-Planning & Grocery recipes · inventory · planner · grocery/Instacart · time feedback · daily UI
↑ returns ↑ BODY-RESPONSE FEEDBACK
"how the meal made me feel" goes back to Track G; "liked it / time was right" stays in Track M

This boundary was already latent in three prior decisions — the abstracted-constraint layer in the schema, the empty PHI envelope on recipe_search, and the C5 feedback split — this page just names it. Practical payoff: Track M stays a plain consumer app (its cloud queries carry zero PHI), and all health-privacy weight concentrates in Track G.

6. Keeping the two pieces distinct — working rules

Status: proposed, not yet ratified. One entanglement exists today: recipes/allergens.py is Track G safety logic living in Track M's package — fine as a stopgap, moves guidance-side when the real ingredient resolver replaces it. Otherwise the split is clean. Rules to keep it that way:

  1. The planner never reads raw clinical atoms — abstracted constraints only. If the constraint vocabulary is too thin, extend the vocabulary, don't reach past the seam.
  2. Track M query types keep empty PHI envelopes. Any PHI need in a planning/grocery query means it belongs to Track G or must be decomposed.
  3. Feedback writes as two paths from birth — no combined feedback table.
  4. Disclosure content is authored by Track G, rendered by Track M — never hardcoded app copy.
  5. New code lands on its side of the seam — guidance logic under knowledge/ (or future guidance/), app logic under recipes/ / inventory/ / future planner/ / grocery/. No retroactive refactors.

Long-term payoff (not a prototype goal): Track M could ship standalone as a plain meal-planning app with a stubbed constraint interface; Track G is the differentiator that plugs in.
