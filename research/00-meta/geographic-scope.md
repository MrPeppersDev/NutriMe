# NutriMe — Geographic Scope

> Two distinct scopes: **product audience** (who the system is built for) and **research scope** (where we research from). They are deliberately different.

## Primary product audience

US, Canada, Western Europe (UK, France, Germany, Netherlands, Belgium, Nordics, Iberia, Ireland, Switzerland, Austria) — households sharing general Western dietary conventions.

This is who the product is built to serve. Most user-facing defaults (units, portion sizes, equipment vocabulary, ingredient availability assumptions, regulatory framing) target this audience.

## Why research scope is broader than audience scope

Research draws from a much broader international scope so the system can offer Western users **horizon-broadening suggestions** from global culinary and nutrition traditions — especially relevant to younger generations more open to international cuisines and more flexible dietary patterns. The asymmetry is deliberate: narrow audience, broad menu.

## Geographic neutrality in evidence surfacing

Per [Constitutional Rule 9](constitutional-rules.md#rule-9--geographic-neutrality-in-evidence-surfacing): **the user's geographic location is a logistics / utility data point, not a content filter.** Authoritative bodies (USDA, CDC, EFSA, NHS, NHMRC, Health Canada, WHO, regional equivalents) are surfaced equal-weighted regardless of where the user is. The system does not default to or prioritize the home-country body's position.

Audience scope (above) defines the population the product is built to serve and shapes utility defaults (units, ingredient availability assumptions, equipment vocabulary). It does not narrow what evidence the user sees or how that evidence is weighted.

## Primary research scope

Sweeps default to comprehensive coverage of these countries:

- **United States**
- **European Union** (treated as a research bloc; EFSA + national EU bodies)
- **United Kingdom** (separate post-Brexit; major Cochrane + NHS research presence)
- **Australia**
- **New Zealand**
- **Canada**
- **China**
- **Japan** (longevity research, large nutrition cohorts, Okinawan + traditional dietary patterns)
- **Korea** (Samsung's home-grown health ecosystem, growing precision-nutrition research)
- **Israel** (Weizmann Institute / Eran Segal's lab — major personalized nutrition center, PREDICT collaborator)
- **Russia** (Soviet-era nutrition science legacy + modern research)

## Opportunistic scope

Beyond the primary list, sweeps include any country where notable work surfaces — academic centers, regulatory bodies, open-source projects, or unique cultural / traditional dietary research. India, Brazil, Nordic countries, Singapore, and others frequently surface here.
